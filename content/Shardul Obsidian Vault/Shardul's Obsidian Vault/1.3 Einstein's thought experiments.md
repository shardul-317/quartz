### Chasing a Beam of Light

At age 16, Einstein imagined what would happen if he could travel alongside a beam of light at the same speed:

- According to classical physics, the light would appear stationary, which contradicts Maxwell's equations.
    
- This paradox helped Einstein realize that the speed of light must be constant for all observers, regardless of their motion.
    

### Train and Lightning Experiment

Einstein used this thought experiment to demonstrate relativity of simultaneity:

- Imagine a train with two lightning bolts striking the front and back simultaneously from the perspective of a platform observer.
    
- A passenger on the train would see the lightning strike closer to them first due to their motion.
    
- This shows that simultaneity is relative and depends on the observer's frame of reference.
    

### Moving Mirror Experiment

Einstein considered a light clock with a photon bouncing between two mirrors:

- In a moving frame, the photon's path appears diagonal compared to a stationary frame.
    
- This thought experiment helps derive the time dilation formula, showing that moving clocks run slower.
    

### Elevator Experiment

This thought experiment laid groundwork for general relativity:

- Imagine being in a sealed elevator; you cannot distinguish between gravitational acceleration and uniform acceleration.
    
- This principle of equivalence shows that gravity can be simulated by acceleration and vice versa.
    

These thought experiments were crucial in developing the theory of Special Relativity and demonstrating how our intuitive notions of space and time must be revised at high speeds.
