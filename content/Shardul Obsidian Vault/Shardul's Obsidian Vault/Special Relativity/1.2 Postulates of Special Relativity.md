### First Postulate

**The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames of reference.**

- This means that no mechanical experiment can distinguish whether you are at rest or moving at constant velocity.
    
- The laws of physics (including mechanics and electromagnetism) take the same form in any inertial frame.
    
- This postulate generalizes Galileo's principle of relativity to include electromagnetic phenomena.
    

### Second Postulate

**The speed of light in a vacuum is constant for all observers, regardless of their motion or the motion of the light source.**

- The speed of light (c) is approximately 3×108 m/s.
    
- This postulate was directly motivated by Maxwell's equations, which predicted a fixed speed of light without specifying a reference frame.
    
- It contradicted the classical velocity addition formula, implying that Newtonian mechanics needed modification at high speeds.
    

### Implications of the Postulates

- **Relativity of simultaneity**: Events simultaneous in one frame may not be simultaneous in another.
    
- **Time dilation**: Moving clocks run slower compared to stationary ones.
    
- **Length contraction**: Objects in motion are measured to be shorter in the direction of motion.
    
- **Mass-energy equivalence**: E=mc2, showing that mass can be converted to energy and vice versa.
