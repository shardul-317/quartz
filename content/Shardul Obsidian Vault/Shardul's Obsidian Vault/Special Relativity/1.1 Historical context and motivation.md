

## The 19th-Century Physics Landscape

In the late 19th century, classical physics was dominated by two major pillars:

1. **Newtonian mechanics**: This provided a framework for understanding motion and forces, based on absolute space and time.
    
2. **Maxwell's equations of electromagnetism**: These described how electric and magnetic fields propagate and interact, predicting that light is an electromagnetic wave traveling at a constant speed c in a vacuum.
    

These two frameworks appeared fundamentally incompatible:

- Newtonian mechanics was based on absolute space and time.
    
- Maxwell's equations predicted a fixed speed of light, seemingly dependent on the observer's motion.
    

### The Ether Hypothesis

To reconcile these theories, physicists proposed the existence of a **luminiferous ether**:

- A stationary medium through which light waves propagated.
    
- Expected to provide an absolute reference frame for measuring motion.
    
- Predicted that light would travel at different speeds depending on the observer's motion relative to the ether.
    

### The Michelson-Morley Experiment (1887)

Albert Michelson and Edward Morley designed an experiment to detect the Earth's motion through the ether:

- Used an interferometer to measure the difference in light speed in different directions.
    
- Expected to find an "ether wind" effect as the Earth moved in its orbit.
    
- Result: No detectable difference in light speed in any direction - a null result that contradicted the ether theory.
    

### Pre-Einstein Interpretations

The null result of Michelson-Morley puzzled physicists:

- Some proposed ad-hoc solutions like length contraction (FitzGerald and Lorentz).
    
- These maintained the ether concept but required physical objects to contract when moving through the ether.
    
- Theoretical patchwork without a coherent foundation.
    

### Einstein's Revolutionary Approach (1905)

Albert Einstein resolved the contradiction by rethinking fundamental assumptions:

- Published "On the Electrodynamics of Moving Bodies" in 1905.
    
- Proposed two postulates that redefined space and time:
    
    1. The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames (principle of relativity).
        
    2. The speed of light in vacuum is constant for all observers (invariance of c).
        

### Significance of the Postulates

Einstein's approach was revolutionary because:

- It treated time as relative rather than absolute.
    
- It made the speed of light an invariant constant.
    
- It unified mechanics and electromagnetism.
    
- It led to predictions that contradicted common sense but were later confirmed experimentally.
