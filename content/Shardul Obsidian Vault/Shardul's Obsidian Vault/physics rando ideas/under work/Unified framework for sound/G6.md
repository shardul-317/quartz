### **G6: Thermoacoustic Effects**

**Objective**: Model sound generation from heat release during combustion and its coupling with acoustic oscillations.

---

##### **3.6.1 Governing Equations**

1. **Energy Equation**:  
   The first law of thermodynamics for a control volume is:  
   $$
   \rho c_p \frac{D T}{D t} = k \nabla^2 T + \dot{q},
$$  
   where $T$ is temperature, $c_p$ is specific heat at constant pressure, $k$ is thermal conductivity, and $\dot{q}$ is the heat release rate per unit volume.

2. **Acoustic Wave Equation with Heat Release**:  
   Coupling the energy equation with the wave equation gives:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{c^2} \frac{\partial \dot{q}}{\partial t},
$$  
   where $\gamma$ is the specific heat ratio.

3. **Rayleigh Criterion**:  
   Thermoacoustic instability occurs when the phase between heat release oscillations and pressure oscillations satisfies:  
   $$
   \int \dot{q}' p' \, dV > 0,
$$  
   where $\dot{q}'$ and $p'$ are the perturbed heat release and pressure.

---

##### **3.6.2 Derivation of the Thermoacoustic Wave Equation**

1. **Start with the Energy Equation**:  
   For small perturbations around a mean flow, expand temperature and heat release:  
   $$
   T = T_0 + T', \quad \dot{q} = \dot{q}_0 + \dot{q}'.
$$  

2. **Linearize the Energy Equation**:  
   Neglecting higher-order terms:  
   $$
   \rho_0 c_p \frac{\partial T'}{\partial t} = k \nabla^2 T' + \dot{q}'.
$$  

3. **Relate Temperature to Pressure**:  
   Using the ideal gas law $p' = \rho_0 c_0^2 T'$, substitute into the energy equation:  
   $$
   \frac{\partial p'}{\partial t} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{c_0^2} \dot{q}'.
$$  

4. **Formulate the Wave Equation**:  
   Substitute into the homogeneous wave equation:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = \frac{\gamma - 1}{c^2} \frac{\partial \dot{q}}{\partial t}.
$$  

---

##### **3.6.3 Case Study: Combustion Instability in Gas Turbines**

1. **Problem Setup**:  
   Model pressure oscillations in a gas turbine combustor due to unsteady heat release.

2. **Parameters**:  
   - Mean pressure: $p_0 = 10^5 \, \text{Pa}$  
   - Heat release rate: $\dot{q} = 10^{12} \, \text{W/m}^3$  
   - Frequency: $f = 100 \, \text{Hz}$  

3. **Calculation**:  
   The pressure oscillation amplitude can be estimated using:  
   $$
   p' \approx \frac{\gamma - 1}{c_0^2} \frac{\partial \dot{q}}{\partial t} \cdot \frac{1}{\omega^2 - \omega_0^2},
$$  
   where $\omega = 2\pi f$ and $\omega_0$ is the system's natural frequency.

4. **Result**:  
   For a combustor with natural frequency $f_0 = 95 \, \text{Hz}$:  
   $$
   p' \approx \frac{0.4}{(343)^2} \times 10^{12} \times \frac{1}{(2\pi \times 100)^2 - (2\pi \times 95)^2} \approx 500 \, \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.6.4 Dimensional Analysis**

1. **Heat Release Term**:  
   $$
   [\dot{q}] = \frac{\text{W}}{\text{m}^3} = \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m} \cdot \text{s}^3}.
$$  

2. **Source Term**:  
   $$
   \left[\frac{\gamma - 1}{c^2} \frac{\partial \dot{q}}{\partial t}\right] = \frac{\text{Pa}}{\text{s}^2}.
$$  

3. **Pressure Term**:  
   $$
   [p] = \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.6.5 Limitations**

1. **Linear Assumption**:  
   The derivation assumes small perturbations and linear acoustics, which may not hold for large-amplitude oscillations.

2. **Simplified Combustion Model**:  
   The model treats heat release as a spatially uniform perturbation, neglecting flame dynamics and chemistry.

3. **Neglected Turbulence**:  
   The model does not account for turbulent fluctuations in heat release, which can be significant in gas turbines.
