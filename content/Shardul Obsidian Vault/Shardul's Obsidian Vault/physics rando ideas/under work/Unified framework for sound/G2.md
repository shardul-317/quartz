### **G2: Periodic Displacement**

**Objective**: Model sound generation from harmonic mechanical forcing using the wave equation.

---

##### **3.2.1 Wave Equation with Harmonic Source**

1. **Homogeneous Wave Equation**:  
   The standard wave equation describes how pressure waves propagate in a medium:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = 0.
$$  
   For periodic sources, we introduce a source term $S(\vec{r}, t)$ to account for the mechanical forcing.

2. **Inhomogeneous Wave Equation**:  
   For a point source undergoing harmonic displacement, the equation becomes:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = S(\vec{r}, t),
$$  
   where $S(\vec{r}, t) = -\rho_0 \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} \left( A \sin(\omega t) \delta(\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0) \right)$.

3. **Harmonic Forcing**:  
   Consider a boundary or object vibrating sinusoidally with displacement:  
   $$
   x(t) = A \sin(\omega t).
$$  
   The acceleration of this motion becomes:  
   $$
   \ddot{x}(t) = -A \omega^2 \sin(\omega t).
$$  
   This acceleration directly drives the pressure field.

---

##### **3.2.2 Green's Function Solution**

1. **Green's Function**:  
   The solution to the inhomogeneous wave equation can be expressed using Green's function $G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t')$, which represents the pressure response at $(\vec{r}, t)$ due to a unit impulse at $(\vec{r}', t')$:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{V} G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') S(\vec{r}', t') \, dV' \, dt'.
$$  

2. **Free-Space Green's Function**:  
   For an unbounded medium, the Green's function is:  
   $$
   G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') = \frac{\delta\left(t - t' - \frac{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}{c}\right)}{4\pi |\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}.
$$  

3. **Substitute Source Term**:  
   Substituting the harmonic source term into the Green's function integral gives:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = -\rho_0 A \omega^2 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{V} \frac{\delta\left(t - t' - \frac{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|}{c}\right)}{4\pi |\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|} \sin(\omega t') \, dV' \, dt'.
$$  

4. **Simplify Integral**:  
   Evaluating the integral leads to:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \frac{\rho_0 A \omega^2}{4\pi |\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|} \cos\left(\omega t - \frac{\omega |\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|}{c}\right).
$$  

---

##### **3.2.3 Resonance in Confined Spaces**

1. **Boundary Conditions**:  
   In confined spaces (e.g., pipes, cavities), pressure waves reflect off boundaries, leading to standing waves. For a pipe of length $L$ with fixed ends, the pressure satisfies:  
   $$
   p(0, t) = p(L, t) = 0.
$$  

2. **Normal Modes**:  
   The solution inside the cavity becomes:  
   $$
   p_n(x, t) = \frac{\rho_0 A \omega^2}{4\pi} \frac{\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)}{\frac{n\pi}{L}} \cos\left(\omega t - \frac{\omega |\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|}{c}\right),
$$  
   where $n$ is the mode number.

3. **Resonance Condition**:  
   Resonance occurs when:  
   $$
   \frac{n\pi c}{L} = \omega.
$$  
   This amplifies the pressure by a factor of $\frac{L}{n\pi}$.

---

##### **3.2.4 Dimensional Analysis**

1. **Pressure Term**:  
   $$
   [p] = \text{Pa} = \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m} \cdot \text{s}^2}.
$$  

2. **Source Term**:  
   $$
   [S] = \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3 \cdot \text{s}^2}.
$$  

3. **Green's Function**:  
   $$
   [G] = \frac{\text{Pa} \cdot \text{s}^2}{\text{m}^3}.
$$  

---

##### **3.2.5 Case Study: Violin String**

1. **Problem Setup**:  
   A violin string of length $L = 0.25$ m vibrates at $\omega = 440$ Hz (A4 note). Calculate the radiated pressure.

2. **Parameters**:  
   - Air density: $\rho_0 = 1.225 \, \text{kg/m}^3$  
   - Speed of sound: $c = 343 \, \text{m/s}$  
   - String displacement: $A = 1 \, \mu\text{m}$  

3. **Calculation**:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \frac{1.225 \times 1 \times 10^{-6} \times (2\pi \times 440)^2}{4\pi |\vec{r}|} \cos\left(2\pi \times 440 t - \frac{2\pi \times 440 |\vec{r}|}{343}\right).
$$  

4. **Result**:  
   At $|\vec{r}| = 1$ m:  
   $$
   p \approx \frac{1.225 \times 10^{-6} \times 440^2}{4 \times 1} \cos\left(440 \times 2\pi t - \frac{880 \pi \times 1}{343}\right) \approx 7.1 \times 10^{-4} \cos(...) \, \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.2.6 Limitations**

1. **Linear Assumption**:  
   The derivation assumes small displacements ($A \ll \lambda$) and linear acoustics.

2. **Free-Space Approximation**:  
   The Green's function solution applies strictly to unbounded domains. Boundary effects require mode summation (as shown for resonance).

3. **Monopole Source**:  
   The derivation assumes a point source. Extended sources (e.g., plates) would require surface integrals of the Green's function.
