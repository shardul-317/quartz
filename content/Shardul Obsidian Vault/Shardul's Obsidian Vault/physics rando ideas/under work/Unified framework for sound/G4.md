### **G4: Impulsive Forces**

**Objective**: Model sound generation from abrupt mechanical motions using the wave equation with impulsive source terms.

---

##### **3.4.1 Wave Equation with Impulsive Source**

1. **Impulsive Source Term**:  
   For sudden motions, the acceleration term in the wave equation becomes impulsive. Consider a point source undergoing a displacement $x(t)$ with jerk $j(t) = \frac{d^3x}{dt^3}$:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = -\rho_0 \nabla \cdot \frac{\partial \vec{a}}{\partial t}.
$$  
   For an impulsive force, $\vec{a}(t) \approx j(t) \Delta t$, leading to:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = -\rho_0 \nabla \cdot \dot{\vec{a}}.
$$  

2. **Delta Function Approximation**:  
   Model the impulsive force as a Dirac delta function in time:  
   $$
   j(t) = J_0 \delta(t),
$$  
   where $J_0$ is the magnitude of the jerk.

---

##### **3.4.2 Green's Function Solution**

1. **Green's Function**:  
   Using the free-space Green's function for the wave equation:  
   $$
   G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') = \frac{\delta\left(t - t' - \frac{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}{c}\right)}{4\pi |\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}.
$$  

2. **Substitute Source Term**:  
   The pressure field becomes:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = -\rho_0 \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{V} G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') \frac{\partial^2 x(t')}{\partial t'^2} \, dV' \, dt'.
$$  

3. **Evaluate Integral**:  
   For a point source at $\vec{r}_0$ with impulsive acceleration:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \frac{\rho_0 J_0 L^2}{4\pi c |\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|} \delta'\left(t - \frac{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}_0|}{c}\right).
$$  
   Here, $L$ is a characteristic length scale of the source.

---

##### **3.4.3 Physical Interpretation**

1. **Jerk as Acoustic Source**:  
   The third derivative of displacement (jerk) directly drives the pressure field. For abrupt motions, the pressure spike is proportional to the rate of change of acceleration.

2. **Example**:  
   A hammer strike on a surface creates a pressure wave with characteristic $\delta'(t)$ behavior, radiating outward at the speed of sound.

---

##### **3.4.4 Dimensional Analysis**

1. **Jerk Term**:  
   $$
   [j] = \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^3}.
$$  

2. **Source Term**:  
   $$
   [-\rho_0 \nabla \cdot \dot{\vec{a}}] = \frac{\text{kg}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot \frac{\text{m}}{\text{s}^3} \cdot \frac{1}{\text{m}} = \frac{\text{Pa}}{\text{s}^2}.
$$  

3. **Pressure Term**:  
   $$
   [p] = \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.4.5 Case Study: Whip Crack**

1. **Problem Setup**:  
   A whip tip accelerates abruptly, creating a pressure wave that propagates through air.

2. **Parameters**:  
   - Jerk magnitude: $J_0 = 10^4 \, \text{m/s}^3$  
   - Characteristic length: $L = 0.1 \, \text{m}$  
   - Distance from source: $r = 1 \, \text{m}$  

3. **Calculation**:  
   $$
   p(r, t) = \frac{1.225 \times 10^4 \times 0.1^2}{4\pi \times 343 \times 1} \delta'\left(t - \frac{1}{343}\right).
$$  

4. **Result**:  
   The pressure spike at 1 m distance is approximately:  
   $$
   p \approx \frac{1.225 \times 10^4 \times 0.01}{4 \times 343} \approx 0.0087 \, \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.4.6 Limitations**

1. **Linear Acoustics**:  
   The derivation assumes small pressure perturbations relative to ambient pressure.

2. **Point Source Approximation**:  
   Extended sources (e.g., plates or structures) would require surface integration of the Green's function.

3. **Neglected Viscosity**:  
   For very short-duration impulses, viscosity may play a significant role in attenuating the pressure wave.
