### **G7: Electromagnetic Sound**

**Objective**: Model sound generation from Lorentz force interactions in conductive fluids or plasmas.

---

##### **3.7.1 Governing Equations**

1. **Momentum Equation with Lorentz Force**:  
   In the presence of electric currents and magnetic fields, the Navier-Stokes equations must include the Lorentz force term:  
   $$
   \rho \left( \frac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t} + \vec{v} \cdot \nabla \vec{v} \right) = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{v} + \vec{J} \times \vec{B},
$$  
   where $\vec{J}$ is the current density and $\vec{B}$ is the magnetic field.

2. **Maxwell's Equations**:  
   The current density $\vec{J}$ is related to the electric field $\vec{E}$ through Ohm's law:  
   $$
   \vec{J} = \sigma (\vec{E} + \vec{v} \times \vec{B}),
$$  
   where $\sigma$ is the electrical conductivity.

3. **Wave Equation with Electromagnetic Source**:  
   Combining these equations leads to an inhomogeneous wave equation for the pressure field:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = \nabla \cdot (\vec{J} \times \vec{B}).
$$  

---

##### **3.7.2 Physical Interpretation**

1. **Lorentz Force as Acoustic Source**:  
   The term $\vec{J} \times \vec{B}$ represents the force per unit volume exerted on the fluid by the electromagnetic fields. This force generates pressure waves through momentum transfer.

2. **Plasma Speakers**:  
   In plasma speakers, high-frequency oscillating currents create time-varying magnetic fields that interact with the plasma's own current, generating pressure waves through the Lorentz force.

---

##### **3.7.3 Dimensional Analysis**

1. **Lorentz Force Term**:  
   $$
   [\vec{J} \times \vec{B}] = \left( \frac{\text{A}}{\text{m}^2} \right) \left( \frac{\text{N}}{\text{A} \cdot \text{m}} \right) = \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}^3}.
$$  

2. **Source Term**:  
   $$
   [\nabla \cdot (\vec{J} \times \vec{B})] = \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}^3} \cdot \frac{1}{\text{m}} = \frac{\text{Pa}}{\text{s}^2}.
$$  

3. **Pressure Term**:  
   $$
   [p] = \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.7.4 Case Study: Plasma Speaker**

1. **Problem Setup**:  
   Model the acoustic output of a plasma speaker driven by oscillating currents.

2. **Parameters**:  
   - Current density: $J = 10^6 \, \text{A/m}^2$  
   - Magnetic field: $B = 1 \, \text{T}$  
   - Frequency: $f = 1 \, \text{kHz}$  

3. **Calculation**:  
   The pressure field can be estimated using:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \frac{1}{4\pi |\vec{r}|} \int \vec{J} \times \vec{B} \, dV.
$$  

4. **Result**:  
   For a small volume element $dV = 10^{-6} \, \text{m}^3$ at a distance of 1 m:  
   $$
   p \approx \frac{1}{4\pi \times 1} \times 10^6 \times 1 \times 10^{-6} = \frac{1}{4\pi} \approx 0.0796 \, \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.7.5 Limitations**

1. **Linear Assumption**:  
   The derivation assumes weak electromagnetic fields and small pressure perturbations.

2. **Ohm's Law**:  
   The model assumes an ideal linear Ohm's law without considering nonlinear effects like Hall currents.

3. **Neglected Viscosity**:  
   The model neglects viscous effects, which may be significant in low-Mach-number flows.
