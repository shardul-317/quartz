### **G5: Viscous Boundary Layers**

**Objective**: Model sound generation from viscous shear layers in fluid flows.

---

##### **3.5.1 Governing Equations**

1. **Navier-Stokes Equations**:  
   The Navier-Stokes equations for incompressible flow are:  
   $$
   \rho \left( \frac{\partial \vec{v}}{\partial t} + \vec{v} \cdot \nabla \vec{v} \right) = -\nabla p + \mu \nabla^2 \vec{v} + \vec{f},
$$  
   where $\mu$ is the dynamic viscosity and $\vec{f}$ represents body forces.

2. **Boundary Layer Approximation**:  
   Near solid surfaces, viscous effects dominate, leading to the formation of boundary layers. The boundary layer equations simplify the Navier-Stokes equations by assuming:  
   - Streamwise velocity $u \gg$ crosswise velocity $v$
   - Pressure variation in the crosswise direction is negligible

3. **Velocity Profile**:  
   The velocity profile in a turbulent boundary layer can be approximated using the law of the wall:  
   $$
   u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa} \ln(y^+) + B,
$$  
   where $u^+ = u / u_*, y^+ = y u_* / \nu, \kappa$ is the von Kármán constant, and $B$ is a constant.

---

##### **3.5.2 Shear Stress and Viscous Dissipation**

1. **Shear Stress**:  
   The shear stress $\tau_{xy}$ in the boundary layer is given by:  
   $$
   \tau_{xy} = \mu \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}.
$$  
   This stress generates noise through viscous dissipation and turbulence.

2. **Viscous Dissipation**:  
   The viscous dissipation term in the energy equation is:  
   $$
   \phi = \mu \left( 2\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right)^2 + 2\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right)^2 \right).
$$  
   This term represents the conversion of kinetic energy into heat and sound.

---

##### **3.5.3 Wave Equation with Viscous Terms**

1. **Viscous Wave Equation**:  
   Incorporating viscous effects into the wave equation:  
   $$
   \nabla^2 p - \frac{1}{c^2} \frac{\partial^2 p}{\partial t^2} = \nabla \cdot \left( \mu \nabla^2 \vec{v} \right).
$$  
   This equation captures the generation of sound waves from viscous shear layers.

2. **Green's Function Solution**:  
   Using the free-space Green's function:  
   $$
   G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') = \frac{\delta\left(t - t' - \frac{|\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}{c}\right)}{4\pi |\vec{r} - \vec{r}'|}.
$$  
   The pressure field can be expressed as:  
   $$
   p(\vec{r}, t) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \int_{V} G(\vec{r}, t; \vec{r}', t') \nabla \cdot \left( \mu \nabla^2 \vec{v}(\vec{r}', t') \right) \, dV' \, dt'.
$$  

---

##### **3.5.4 Dimensional Analysis**

1. **Viscous Term**:  
   $$
   [\mu \nabla^2 \vec{v}] = \text{Pa} \cdot \text{s} \cdot \text{m}^{-2}.
$$  

2. **Source Term**:  
   $$
   [\nabla \cdot (\mu \nabla^2 \vec{v})] = \text{Pa} \cdot \text{s}^{-2}.
$$  

3. **Pressure Term**:  
   $$
   [p] = \text{Pa}.
$$  

---

##### **3.5.5 Case Study: HVAC System Noise**

1. **Problem Setup**:  
   Model noise generation in an HVAC system due to turbulent flow in ducts.

2. **Parameters**:  
   - Air viscosity: $\mu = 1.8 \times 10^{-5} \, \text{Pa·s}$  
   - Flow velocity: $U = 5 \, \text{m/s}$  
   - Duct dimensions: $0.3 \, \text{m} \times 0.3 \, \text{m}$  

3. **Calculation**:  
   The boundary layer thickness $\delta$ can be estimated using:  
   $$
   \delta \approx \frac{5 \nu}{U},
$$  
   where $\nu = \mu / \rho$ is the kinematic viscosity.

4. **Result**:  
   The calculated boundary layer thickness helps determine the velocity profile and shear stress distribution, which can be used to predict the noise spectrum generated by the HVAC system.

---

##### **3.5.6 Limitations**

1. **Turbulent Flow Assumption**:  
   The boundary layer equations assume fully developed turbulent flow, which may not hold for transitional or laminar flows.

2. **Geometry Limitations**:  
   The model assumes simple geometries (e.g., flat plates, circular ducts). Complex geometries require more advanced numerical methods.

3. **Frequency Range**:  
   The model is most accurate for low to medium frequency noise. High-frequency noise may require consideration of additional mechanisms.
